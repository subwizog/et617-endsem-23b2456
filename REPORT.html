D1: Educational Problem

1. Problem Description

Class 11 physics students routinely treat the horizontal projectile range formula $R = \frac{v^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$ as an abstract plug-and-chug equation rather than an emergent consequence of orthogonal velocity components and time of flight. This textbook-drill conditioning leaves two persistent misconceptions intact: the belief that 45° is universally optimal regardless of launch height, and that doubling launch speed merely doubles horizontal range. For high-achieving Olympiad aspirants who are bored by repetitive formula substitution, standard practice fails to develop genuine experimental intuition. They require a rigorous, challenge-based inquiry laboratory where they must formulate hypotheses, conduct controlled single-variable experiments, observe trajectory ghosts, and resolve cognitive conflicts when their predictions fail under non-trivial boundary conditions.

2. Measurable Learning Outcomes

  1. Predict the change in horizontal projectile range resulting from doubling launch speed at a fixed angle, and verify through experimental trials that range scales quadratically ($R \propto v^2$) rather than linearly.
  2. Compare trajectories of complementary launch angles ($\theta$ and $90^\circ - \theta$) on level ground, and explain using mathematical symmetry why both angles achieve the identical horizontal range.
  3. Design a controlled, single-variable experiment and justify analytically why launching from an elevated cliff ($h > 0$) causes the range-maximizing angle to drop below 45° ($\theta^* < 45^\circ$).

3. Context of Use

Evening study on personal Android smartphone (360 px width screen), operated primarily with one thumb while resting or commuting in 10–15 minute focused problem-solving bursts between competitive Olympiad mock sets. Assumptions: Flat landing surface, uniform downward gravitational field ($g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$), negligible air resistance (explicitly stated on the About screen).

D3: Educational Content

1. Conceptual Framework & Physics Model

The horizontal range $R$ of a projectile launched at angle $\theta$ with speed $v$ from height $h$ above a flat surface is governed by the independent decomposition of horizontal and vertical motions: $$v_x = v \cos\theta, \quad v_y = v \sin\theta$$ Vertical displacement is: $$y(t) = h + v_y t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$$ Setting $y(T) = 0$ yields the total time of flight $T$: $$T = \frac{v_y + \sqrt{v_y^2 + 2gh}}{g}$$ Horizontal range is: $$R = v_x T = \frac{v \cos\theta \left(v \sin\theta + \sqrt{v^2\sin^2\theta + 2gh}\right)}{g}$$ On level ground ($h = 0$), $R = \frac{v^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$, which yields identical ranges for complementary angles and quadratic scaling with speed. On an elevated cliff ($h > 0$), differentiating $R$ with respect to $\theta$ yields the optimal angle $\theta^* = \arctan\left(\frac{v}{\sqrt{v^2 + 2gh}}\right) < 45^\circ$.

2. Multi-Format Tasks & Feedback Design

TaskFormatObjective & Feedback
1. The Bullseye MCQ Hypothesis Predict how many angles can hit a 60 m target. Model: Exactly two ($\approx 35^\circ$ and $55^\circ$, adding to $90^\circ$). Feedback confirms $\sin 2\theta$ symmetry.
2. Double Trouble Numeric Prediction Given baseline $v=15\text{ m/s} \to 22.6\text{ m}$, predict range at $v=30\text{ m/s}$. Model: $\approx 90.5\text{ m}$ ($4\times$). M2 feedback shows prediction vs reality without spoiling $v^2$.
3. The Cliff Shot Interactive Virtual Lab Find optimal angle from 20 m cliff at 10 m/s. Model: $\approx 24.3^\circ$ ($22.6\text{ m}$), beating $45^\circ$ ($20.3\text{ m}$). M1 feedback prompts why $45^\circ$ was beaten.
4. Spot the Flaw Controlled Experiment MCQ Identify confounding when both angle and speed change simultaneously. Model: Change one variable at a time.
5. Olympiad Stretch Analytical Justification Text Find optimal angle for $h=30\text{ m}, v=15\text{ m/s}$ ($\approx 27.7^\circ$) and justify why $\theta^* \to 0^\circ$ as $h \to \infty$. Evaluated via Socratic AI.

D5: Two Contrasting Learner Scenarios

Scenario A: Correct Learner Path (Deductive Master)

In Challenge 2, the learner recognizes that $R = \frac{v^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$. Given the baseline of $22.6\text{ m}$ at $15\text{ m/s}$, she calculates $(2)^2 \times 22.6 = 90.4\text{ m}$ and inputs 90.5. In the Lab, she launches at $30\text{ m/s}$ and observes $90.5\text{ m}$. The system recognizes the exact match, skips the Support Ladder entirely, marks Challenge 2 complete, and presents the Olympiad Extension Harder Follow-Up: "If you triple launch speed (3×), by what factor will range increase?"

Scenario B: Misconception Learner Path (M2 Speed Doubling & Support Ladder)

In Challenge 2, the learner assumes linear proportionality and enters 45.2 m. In the Lab, she launches at $30\text{ m/s}$ and observes $90.5\text{ m}$. The system flags M2 and strictly hides $v^2$, stating: "Prediction Mismatch: You predicted 45.2 m, but the ball reached 90.5 m. Enter the Support Ladder to investigate." The learner progresses through Rung 1 (inspect gap), Rung 2 (Socratic reflection), Rung 3 (controlled speed sweep from 10–35 m/s showing upward parabolic curve without peak giveaways), and Rung 4 (revises hypothesis). Upon retesting in the Lab, completion is unlocked.

D7: Pedagogical Rationale

Constructivist inquiry requires cognitive conflict: observing the projectile overshoot 45 m to land at 90.5 m forces the learner to dismantle linear assumptions. In response to the Problem 5 design challenge, the 4-step Support Ladder provides structured scaffolding (gap naming → Socratic questioning → controlled variable sweep graph without peak callouts → hypothesis revision) without ever revealing the answer prematurely. Constraint C3 ensures one-handed mobile ergonomics via large $54\times 48\text{ px}$ steppers, while high-achieving Olympiad learners (L6) bypass scaffolding when answering correctly to receive advanced extension challenges.
